1        INTRODUCTION


1.1.1.   This Planning Statement has been prepared by WSP on behalf of Power Living (Barclay House)
         Limited. The purpose of this Planning Statement is to assist Manchester City Council (the Council) in
         the consideration of a planning application at Barclay House for;
         “Erection of a two-storey rooftop extension to form 9 duplex apartments and the conversion
         of the existing redundant internal car park to create a further 4 duplex apartments at Barclay
         House, Whitworth Street West, Manchester”
1.1.2.   This statement provides an overview of the site and its wider context, commenting on relevant
         planning history and planning policy frameworks against which this application should be
         determined. The statement makes a planning assessment of the proposed scheme, demonstrating
         the site is suitable for a rooftop extension and internal conversion to form new residential
         apartments.
1.1.3.   The application comprises the erection of 13 duplex apartments through a two-storey rooftop
         extension and the conversion of the existing redundant internal car park at Barclay House,
         Whitworth Street West, Manchester.
1.1.4.   This application follows a recent prior approval (Ref: 118033/P3OPA/2017) for the change of use of
         the building from B1(a) offices to residential (C3) (excluding the basement), which was granted by
         Manchester City Council in January 2018. The decision notice is attached at Appendix 1. The
         consent has been implemented and works to the building are currently being undertaken.
1.1.5.   The proposal provides an opportunity to deliver an additional 13 sustainably located apartments
         within the city centre. Barclay House itself is currently being converted to residential apartments with
         the wider area being the subject of significant growth in the form of new residential apartments as
         part of the city’s continued shift to city centre living.
1.1.6.   This Planning Statement is structured as follows:
          Section 2 sets out the site context and the planning history associated with the application site;
          Section 3 outlines the proposed development;
          Section 4 sets out the relevant planning policies at a local and national level;
          Section 5 provides the case for development and explains why the principle of development is
           acceptable;
          Section 6 assesses technical considerations; and
          Section 7 sets out the conclusions.

2        SITE AND SURROUNDINGS


2.1.1.   Barclay House is a 5-storey building located on Whitworth Street West. Until 2019 the building
         comprised mainly office space with ground floor commercial units. However, the building is currently
         being converted to residential apartments under consent 118033/P3OPA/2017.
2.1.2.   The building has a classical appearance and is an example of industrial architecture of the early
         20th century, mimicking the formal composition of civic buildings. The building features a warehouse
         design with double height arches and decorative detail to the main entrance.
2.1.3.   The side and rear elevations demonstrate a transition to a more simplified functional appearance
         with a clear contrast in design from the main façade fronting Whitworth Street.
2.1.4.   The application site is bound by Whitworth Street West to the south, an arm of the Manchester canal
         to north with residential apartments to the east and west. The wider area is characterised by a range
         of uses such as restaurants, cafes, bars, offices, hotels and residential apartments, reflective of its
         inner-city location.
2.1.5.   The site is sustainably located in the heart of Manchester City Centre with an extensive range of
         local services, leisure facilities, shops, employment opportunities and public transport all located
         within walking distance. Oxford Road Train Station lies adjacent to the building with the station
         entrance less than a two-minute walk from Barclay House.
2.1.6.   The surrounding area has a number of buildings, both existing and currently under construction of
         considerable scale. Indeed, the surrounding area has a dense character with a number of buildings
         with large footprints and considerable height.
2.1.7.   In terms of neighbouring buildings, the apartment block immediately to the east of Barclay House is
         10 storeys in height with the adjacent apartment block, One Cambridge Street, rising to 28 storeys.
         Hotspur House also has permission for a 28 storey tower. There are numerous buildings in the
         vicinity that rise to over 10 storeys.
2.1.8.   The building is not listed and does not lie within a Conservation Area, although, there are various
         listed buildings, canal locks and railway viaducts in the vicinity. The Whitworth Street Conservation
         Area lies to the east with the St Peters Square Conservation Area to the north

3        PROPOSED DEVELOPMENT


3.1.1.   The description of development is as follows;
         “Erection of a two-storey rooftop extension to form 9 duplex apartments and the conversion
         of the existing redundant internal car park to create a further 4 duplex apartments at Barclay
         House, Whitworth Street West, Manchester”.
3.1.2.   The proposed development comprises a two-storey rooftop extension in addition to the conversion
         of the existing internal car park and will provide 13 residential apartments comprising;
          5no 2 bed duplex apartments;
          6no 3 bed duplex apartments; and
          2no M2 compliant 3 bed apartments.
3.1.3.   All apartments will comply with the Nationally Described Space Standards (NDSS) and two of the
         proposed three-bedroom apartments will be included to M4(2) Category standards for “accessible
         and adaptable dwellings”.
         Conversion
3.1.4.   The existing car park is currently redundant and is not relied upon or included with the Prior
         Approval office to residential scheme. The ceiling height of the car parking area is greater than the
         first floor ceiling height, facilitating the conversion of this part of the building into four duplex
         apartments.
         Internal works to the existing building will deliver a high quality conversion which is considered to be
         efficient use of space, providing duplex apartments in a highly sustainable location.
3.1.5.   The apartments will feature living space in the form of living room/kitchen on the ground floor
         alongside a bathroom and storage space. The mezzanine level accommodates bedrooms and an
         additional bathroom.
3.1.6.   The apartments would enjoy pleasant views to the north overlooking the Rochdale Canal to the
         north.
         Rooftop Extension
3.1.7.   The proposed roof top extension will create 9 duplex apartments set over two floors. The proposed
         extension will be stepped back from the parapet of Barclay House, with the roof sloping inwardly
         towards the centre of the building in order to minimise its impact on the street scene and Barclay
         House itself. The full design rationale is proposed is set out within the Design and Access Statement
         (DAS).
3.1.8.   The apartments are designed as zoned rooms set over two levels. Generous air spaces have been
         created in the roof space and offer a unique spatial relationship within each living unit benefiting
         from a high level of amenity and outlook. Occupiers will benefit from vistas of the city which provides
         excellent views for future occupiers.

4.1.2.   The current Development Plan for this area comprises the Manchester Core Strategy (adopted July
         2012, plan period to 2027) and the ‘saved’ Policies of the Unitary Development Plan (UDP) (adopted
         July 1995). The site is located within the City Centre area.

          National Planning Policy Framework (2019);

         NATIONAL POLICY
         National Planning Policy Framework
4.1.4.   The National Planning Policy Framework (NPPF) as revised in 2019 is a key material consideration
         in the determination of planning applications. It sets out the Government’s planning policies for
         England and sets out how these policies should be applied. The most pertinent elements of the
         NPPF are discussed below.
         Achieving sustainable development
4.1.5.   The NPPF sets out upfront in paragraphs 7 and 8 that the purpose of the planning system is to
         contribute to the achievement of sustainable development and that sustainable development
         consists of an economic objective, a social objective and an environmental objective. The delivery
         of this sustainable development should be carried out in a way which responds appropriately to local
         character, needs and opportunities.
4.1.6.   The NPPF is built upon a presumption in favour of sustainable development and that development
         proposals which accord with an up-to-date development plan should be approved without delay, as
         set out in paragraph 11.
         Delivering new homes
4.1.7.   The NPPF sets out in paragraph 59 that significantly boosting the supply of homes is a key
         Government objective. This relies on a sufficient amount and variety of land coming forward, which
         includes the important contribution of small and medium sites. It is recognised in paragraph 68 that
         these smaller sites are often built-out relatively quickly, thereby delivering new homes for local areas
         more swiftly.
         Making effective use of land
4.1.8.   The NPPF describes a range of principles for supporting development of land in order to make the
         best and most effective use of land. This includes the requirement for policies and decisions to
         support the development of under-utilised land within settlements for homes in paragraph 118.
4.1.9.   Paragraph 118(e) goes further stating that opportunities to use the airspace above existing
         residential and commercial premises for new homes should be supported. In particular, decision

        DEVELOPMENT PLAN
        Manchester Core Strategy
4.1.12. Policy SP 1 (Spatial Principles) sets out the key spatial principles that will guide development in
        Manchester up to 2027. The Regional Centre will be the focus for economic and commercial
        development, retail, leisure and cultural activity, alongside high quality city living. Moreover, all
        development must have regard to the character of the regeneration area in which it is situated. The
        policy also provides several principles in which developments should adhere to, including:
         Positive contribution to neighbourhoods of choice;
         Reuse previously developed land where possible; and
         Locate development in areas that have good access to jobs, education and sustainable transport.
4.1.13. Policy CC 3 (Housing) states that the City Centre will see the most intensive development of
        housing in the City. It is expected that a minimum of 16,500 new units will be provided from 2010-

        will encourage high standard accommodation which offers a mix of rooms and sizes to suit a range
        of occupants.
4.1.14. Policy CC 6 (City Centre High Density Development) sets out that development in the City Centre
        will generally be high-density and maximise efficiency.
4.1.15. Policy CC 8 (Change and Renewal) states that the Council will support schemes that are likely to
        contribute to the promotion or improvement of the social, economic or environmental well-being of
        Manchester. New development should fully exploit opportunities to contribute to the improvement of
        the City Centre in terms of character and function, taking account of other policies in the Core
        Strategy.
4.1.16. Policy CC 9 (Design and Heritage) requires the design of new buildings to be developed to the
        highest standard in terms of appearance and function. Development in Manchester City Centre
        should preserve or enhance the heritage assets that have been identified, including listed buildings,

         PRINCIPLE OF DEVELOPMENT
5.1.3.   The principle of residential development has been established following approval of application (Ref:
         118033/P3OPA/2017) for the conversion of Barclay House from B1(a) offices (now Class E) to 90
         residential apartments. The consent has been implemented with the conversion itself currently
         taking place. Barclay House should be considered a residential building in its entirety, aside from the
         remaining commercial uses at ground floor.
5.1.4.   Barclay House lies within a highly sustainable city centre location, being located in close proximity to
         an extensive variety of local services and facilities in addition to a range of public transport options.
         Oxford Road Train Station lies adjacent to the building and as such the site demonstrates a very
         high level of sustainability.
5.1.5.   Policy CC3 of the Manchester Core Strategy supports new housing in the city centre setting out that
         the city will see the most intensive development of housing in the City with a minimum of 16,500
         new units provided from 2010-2027.
5.1.6.   Indeed, the supporting text underpinning policy CC3 identifies that;
         “Housing has become an increasingly significant component of Manchester City Centre, and its role
         is expected to grow throughout the plan period. Household projections and demographic trends
         suggest that the demand for flats is likely to be considerable in Manchester. The City Centre will
         therefore accommodate around 28% of the new housing units to be provided up to 2027”.
5.1.7.   Furthermore, policy CC6 sets out that high density development will be supported in the city centre
         specifically commenting that scale, massing, and height of development in the city will significantly
         exceed those approved in other parts of Manchester.
5.1.8.   The proposal would deliver 13 well designed apartments to boost housing delivery within the city
         centre, supporting the Council’s ambitions to increase city centre living.
5.1.9.   The proposal receives further support from policy H1 which sets out that 90% of residential
         development will be on previously developed land. It confirms that new developments should take
         advantage of existing buildings where appropriate through refurbishment or rebuilding works.
5.1.10. Given the proposed development includes both conversion and new build elements it clearly
        receives support from Manchester City Council’s strategic residential polices.
5.1.11. It should be noted that the Council have previously approved a one storey extension at Barclay
        House in January 1970. Whilst the consent was not implemented, it further demonstrates that the
        principle of an extension at Barclay House is acceptable in principle.

5.1.12. The NPPF is also supportive of upward extensions for residential development setting out at

        “Support opportunities to use the airspace above existing residential and commercial premises for
        new homes. In particular, they should allow upward extensions where the development would be

        maintain safe access and egress for occupiers.”
