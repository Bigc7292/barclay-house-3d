INTRODUCTION
This Design and Access Statement has been prepared to support a full planning
application for the proposed conversion of the existing internal and redundant car park
into residential accommodation, and the construction of a rooftop extension.

This document explores the context, background, underlying architectural / urban
design concepts and the evolution of the proposed scheme at Barclay House, 35
Whitworth Street West, Manchester, M1 5NG.

This statement considers the site, its surroundings and all material considerations,
which we consider relevant to the determination of the planning application. It
concludes that planning permission should be granted, having regard to the
development plan and other material considerations.



PROJECT DESCRIPTION
The proposals include the conversion to residential use of the internal and redundant car
parking area at ground floor level, located to the rear of the building, and the construction
of a two-storey roof top extension to provide residential accommodation.

THE NATIONAL PLANNING POLICY FRAMEWORK
The National Planning Policy Framework (the NPPF) was published in March 2012 and revised in February 2019; it constitutes guidance for local planning authorities
and decision makers. Applications for planning permission must be determined in accordance with the local development plan, unless it is silent, or material
considerations indicate otherwise. The NPPF is a material consideration.

Section 16 of the revised NPPF deals with conserving and enhancing the historic environment, in paragraphs 184 to 202. The NPPF defines heritage assets in Annex
2 as:

 “A building, monument, site, place, area or landscape identified as having a degree of significance meriting consideration in planning decisions, because of its
heritage interest. It includes designated heritage assets and assets identified by the local planning authority (including local listing).”

The NPPF places much emphasis on heritage “significance”, which it defines in Annex 2 as:

"The value of a heritage asset to this and future generations because of its heritage interest. That interest may be archaeological, architectural, artistic or historic.
Significance derives not only from a heritage asset’s physical presence, but also from its setting."


Paragraph 184 of the NPPF encourages local planning authorities to recognise that heritage assets are an irreplaceable resource and conserve them in a manner
appropriate to their significance. One of the factors to be considered is the desirability of sustaining and enhancing the significance of heritage assets and putting
them to viable uses consistent with their conservation. Paragraph 185 recognises the fact that new development can make a positive contribution to local character
and distinctiveness.

Paragraph 189 of the NPPF requires applicants to describe the significance of any heritage assets affected by a proposal to a proportionate level of detail. Paragraph
190 requires essentially the same from local planning authorities: to identify and assess the “particular significance” of any heritage asset. It is the significance of the
heritage asset that should be taken into account when considering the impact of a proposal.

According to paragraph 192 of the NPPF, several considerations should be considered, first of which is the desirability of sustaining and enhancing the significance of
heritage assets and putting them to viable uses consistent with their conservation. The paragraph reiterates the well-established concept that new development can
make a positive contribution to local character and distinctiveness.

According to paragraph 197 of the NPPF, the effect of an application on the significance of a non-designated heritage asset should be taken into account in
determining applications. A balanced judgement is required, having regard to the scale of the harm or loss, and the significance of the heritage asset.


The Government is committed to a plan-led system of development control, which was given statutory force by Section 38(6) of the 2004 Planning and Compulsory
Purchase Act. Where an adopted or approved development plan contains relevant policies, Section 38(6) requires that an application for planning permission or an
appeal shall be determined in accordance with the plan, unless material considerations indicate otherwise.

In Manchester, the relevant development plan is the Core Strategy Development Plan Document 2012-2027 (the “Core Strategy") adopted in July 2012 and the saved
policies from the Manchester Unitary Development Plan (UDP), adopted in July 1995.
The NPPF articulates the priorities of the Plan for Growth within planning policy. The NPPF introduces a new ‘presumption’ in favour of sustainable development and
supports proposals that are in accordance with policies in an up to date Development Plan. Sustainable development is about positive growth – making economic,
environmental and social progress for this and future generations.

Pursuing sustainable development involves seeking positive improvements in the quality of the built, natural and historic environment, as well as in people’s quality of
life, including (but not limited to):

      making it easier for jobs to be created in cities, towns and villages;
      moving from a net loss of bio-diversity to achieving net gains for nature;
      replacing poor design with better design;
      improving the conditions in which people live, work, travel and take leisure; and
      widening the choice of high quality homes.

The NPPF establishes a set of core planning principles, which underpin both plan-making and decision-taking. These set out that planning should:

      be genuinely plan-led;
      proactively drive and support sustainable economic development to deliver the homes, business and industrial units, infrastructure and thriving local places that
       the country needs;
      always seek to secure high quality design and a good standard of amenity;
      take account of the different roles and character of different areas;
      support the transition to a low carbon future in a changing climate and encourage the reuse of existing resources;
      encourage the effective use of land by reusing land that has been previously developed; and,
      promote mixed use developments, and encourage multiple benefits from the use of land.

The proposed roof top extension has evolved over time following discussions with the planning department, research into the style of architecture and building materials within the vicinity of

DESIGN MOTTO
The overall principle is simple — to enhance and preserve the building.

Those two key words, ‘enhance’ and ‘preserve’, can be an either/or measure, but it is a key anchor of our design scheme.

      improve the existing building, and overall impression of the area

      help to preserve what is there

The truth of the matter is that this is a significant building project, whether it is a new build or a remodel, then it’s likely to be put more in the ‘enhance’ rather than ‘preserve’ category. This means
that we need to carefully assess what’s already there, not just the existing building but the buildings in the vicinity of Barclay House, and how the new design measures up against the existing.

FOUR STOREY OPTION
The initial design concept at the beginning of the project was to create a
four-storey extension on top of the roof. Three variations for the material
finish and fenestration of the proposed elevations were proposed, CGI’s
of these designs are shown later in this statement.

The proposed four-storey roof extension was proposed in two steps, each
one compromising two storeys. The first extension was recessed 2.0m
from the front facade at Whitworth Street West to diminish the visual
impact from the street below, and lessen the scale of the extension on the
street scene. On the northern facade facing the canal, the extension
would go right to the boundary of the existing building, and match the
current aesthetics of the existing facade to minimise the visual impact of
the extension. The layout of the four storey extension was designed so
that the second part of the proposal would be located next to the canal for
two main reasons; firstly to set the apartments as far as possible from the
traffic noise emanating from Whitworth Street West, and secondly to
reduce the visual impact of the building from Whitworth Street West; its
primary facade.

The same recession from the canal frontage was proposed for the same
principals as the first step/extension was set back from the front facade,
to lower the visual impact and to emphasise the hierarchy of the existing
building and the proposed contemporary extension.

The proposal therefore attempted to provide a high quality extension being
coherent with the history and aesthetics of the existing building offering a
sequential change from the historical to the contemporary.

The first step, including floors five and six would compromise of 8 No.
apartments, in varying sizes from one-bedroom apartments to three
bedroom apartments.

The second step consisted of four residential units on each floor, offering
eight two-bedroom penthouses creating 24 No. units with this proposed
extension, including a communal amenity space for residents.

DESIGN EVOLUTION - INITIAL TWO STOREY ROOFTOP EXTENSION OPTIONS


Following a meeting with the council, the height of the proposed rooftop extension was reduced from four storeys to two storeys, and the design of the elevations and material palette reviewed.
Following the meeting, three new concepts were produced to provide an alternative to the previous four-storey concept proposed for the roof extension. Below are CGI’s of the proposed
designs with a short description of the design philosophy.




Corten Extrusion Option
The form for this design was directed by the proposed positioning of the
duplex penthouse windows. The idea was to accentuate the framing of
the windows as a crisp line, which also canopied over the balcony. The
extensions of the Corten frames when seen from the road appear as the
pinnacle and crowning of the newly refurbished Barclay House. The use
of Corten is in direct relationship to the neighbouring proposed
development and the Corten extension of Oxford Road railway station.
Stone Extrusion Option
This design was based on the same narrative as the Corten Extrusion design explained above, except the Corten has been replaced with stone. The use of stone in
this proposal tries to work in a more holistic manner with the rest of the existing building. This is a more subtle approach especially when seen from the street, creating
a powerful road presence.
Undulating Timber Option
The final concept was an exciting dynamic play of a fluid timber structure, the seamless flow of timber and the curved structure creates a soft edition to the building
design. Powerful and eye catching especially from the street, this design would create a landmark status for the building, a new identity.

DESIGN EVOLUTION - FINAL DESIGN

Following further discussions with the council’s planning officers’, the Client and the design team it was agreed that the earlier roof top extension schemes were too overbearing to
the existing building and the surrounding area, taking away from the existing façade of Barclay House, and as such a new design approach was initiated.

The historic environment is a shared resource of this building and the area. It has been shaped by people responding to the surroundings they inherited, and embodies the aspirations,
skills and investment of successive generations. It reflects the knowledge, beliefs and traditions of this community giving a distinctiveness and a source of identity as a social and
economic asset. During the design process, we considered, of course, changes in the historic environment, which is an inevitable process of the social, economic and technological
changes over the years.

The proposed project has been designed to be sustainable, and in accordance within its significance and values, trying to increase and sustain those values for present and future
generations. The proposed design will effectively be part of the historical development of this area, increasing the quality of living, and giving a re-fresh, and an improvement to the
context surrounding the property, perfectly in accordance with its historical approach. The extra apartments and the new design itself (currently appearing just like a traditional Victorian
slate roof) will preserve and enhance the top aspect of Barclay House, bringing a new injection in the requalification.

ACCESS
The proposals will create an inclusive environment by ensuring a level access entrance and provisions of fully accessible lifts will mean that all floors will be universally accessible without the
need of supervision or assistance.

Barclay House is located in a sustainable location and can be accessed from Whitworth Street and from the rear of the building adjacent to the canal. A proximity access control system is
proposed for the main entrances to provide electronic access through communal entrance door sets. Depending on the final system selected, access will most likely be operated using a card or
key fob, which will be issued to residents and staff members, contractors and postal delivery services to enable access to and from the building.

A central staircase and two internal lifts provide access to the upper floors of the proposed conversion and the roof top extension. Each apartment will be accessed off a network of corridors on
each floor; the corridors link the apartments to the central lobby, staircase and lifts, providing appropriate access to all parts of the building for all residents and visitors.

At least 10% of the proposed apartments within the roof top extension have been designed to be accessible and adaptable dwellings, compliant with the Manchester City Council’s guidelines.
As explained below this will provide accessible living accommodation for disabled residents and visitors, and have been designed to enable the future installation of specialist equipment to
provide disabled access within the apartment. Access to all of them will be granted from the main lifts, which can be reached through the proposed disabled platform lifts that will enable wheel
chairs to get to the main lobby where the lifts are located.

Two access routes into Barclay House are proposed. One access route is proposed through the existing main front entrance door located on Whitworth Street (Route A). A second proposed
access route is proposed to the right-hand side of the main building (Route B). Route B will provide access through the side entrance adjacent to the cycle and bin store, this access will lead
into the building and up to the main lobby area. Both of the proposed access routes will provide stairs and a platform lift to ensure disabled people and wheelchair users have easy access to and
from the building.

Route A proposes access from the main street through the front door of Barclay House. A platform lift is proposed at the front of the building to the side of the existing stairs. The platform will
fold down on the top of the stairs whilst not in use, and can be called from street level, and from the main lobby level to provide wheelchair access from street level at 100.10m up into the main
lobby at 102.20m height, and vice versa. As previously mentioned the platform lift will usually remain at the top of the stairs to keep it secure, and to ensure it is not an obstruction at street level.
The lift will only be at street level whilst in use to allow users to get on or off the lift before it can be sent back to the top of the stairs. Once within the building in the main lobby, a level route of
around 11.0m leads to the secure lift lobby; from here, the entire building can be accessed including the adaptable dwellings on the proposed roof extension.

Route B proposes an additional access located to the side of the building enabling access into the rear lobby area; the gate into the car park was formerly located, here. The ground slopes
slightly from street level up to the access door of the proposed side entrance. The proposed access from street level to Barclay House is a length of approximately 14.0m, with a maximum
gradient of 1:18 from the street level height of 100.10m up to the finished floor level of the existing car park at 100.86m height. This gradient is more than acceptable, and will reduce the internal
height difference from the lower level where the cycle and bin store are located. From the side entrance door to the base of the stairs is an internal corridor with a level section measuring
approximately 5.0m in length; this provides access through the internal corridor and into the bin and cycle store. From this side entrance, the internal corridor runs to the base of the stairs
leading to the Landing and Access Lobby. These stairs have eight risers and will have a platform lift installed to provide wheelchair access up to the landing, lobby access and main entrance
hall. The proposed platform lift will be folded away on top of the stairs to ensure that it does not create an obstruction when not in use, but can be easily accessed as and when required. The
platform lift will be able to be called form the top or bottom of the stairs at any time. A level corridor measuring approximately 14.0m in length provides access from the landing area at the top of
the rear stairs to the secure lift lobby, from here the entire building can be accessed, including the adaptable dwellings on the roof extension. The proposed second route (Route B) will provide
easy access to and from the building, and will provide access for refuse disposal without leaving the building premises for all residents including disabled and wheelchair users.

Level and easy access to all the adaptable dwellings will be granted from the lift in the main lobby. The main entrance to the duplexes will be through the lower floor level, where accessible
toilets are provided. The two proposed access routes (Route A & B) will grant access to the building at all times, ensuring in the unlikely event of a breakdown of one of the proposed disabled
lifts, there will be another lift available, as well as providing alternative egress and access points to Barclay House.

CYCLING & WALKING
The proposal includes a dedicated secure cycle storage area of around 44.8m² on the ground floor located to the east of the building with direct access onto a small side street, which connects
with Whitworth Street West. Providing the residents with quick easy access to and from Whitworth Street and on to the numerous cycling routes in and around Manchester city centre.

In addition, the site lies immediately adjacent to the large pedestrian walkways off Whitworth Street West and a pedestrian crossing with traffic lights is located immediately outside the front of
the building, enabling residents to safely access the transport nodes located in the immediate vicinity of the site. Furthermore, the site’s proximity to the Rochdale Canal Tow Path provides
residents with further flexibility concerning the choice of pedestrian and cycling options to travel around Manchester.

WASTE STORAGE
A bin / recycling store is located to the north east of Barclay House, on the ground floor. The waste storage area measures approximately 42.5m² and can accommodate at least 14 No. 1,100
litre capacity Eurobin’s. This store will permit the storage of bins and the separation of waste for recycling. Doors from the waste store open directly onto a short side street, which connects to
Whitworth Street West, meaning the bins can be removed, emptied and returned without being left on the street for collection. A standard refuse vehicle can reverse up to the entrance of the bin
store directly from Whitworth Street West, stopping to allow the bins to be removed from the store, emptied and returned to the store immediately after emptying.

SUSTAINABILITY
In terms of the buildings environmental sustainability, the residential development is located in a sustainable location close to the centre of Manchester. A ‘fabric first’ approach will be
undertaken throughout this project, looking to improve the thermal envelope of the building and to ensure the development achieves a 15% improvement over Part L of the current Building
Regulations. The apartments will have appropriate passive control measures to limit the effect of solar gains on indoor temperatures in summer. The proposed light fittings will be low energy
efficient fittings. Photovoltaic (PV) panels utilise the sun's energy to generate electricity. The roof of Barclay House provides an ideal location to house solar panels. Initially, at least 50m² of
Photovoltaic (PV) panels are proposed on the roof of Barclay House to provide energy for the new apartments proposed within this application.

To increase the energy efficiency of the development the following elements are proposed:

      High performance glazing to reduce solar and transmission gains
      Improved thermal transmittance U-valued
      Natural ventilation
      Electric Heating and Hot water.
      100% Low energy lighting through the dwellings
      Low flow aerated showerheads and basin tap inserts to reduce domestic water consumption
      Reducing surface water discharge
      Reducing mains/potable water via water efficient devices and equipment
      Inclusion of Photovoltaic Panels on the roof.

VENTILATION
The ventilation system for the proposed apartments will be designed and specified by specialists to comply with Part F of the current edition of the building regulations.
CONCLUSION:
The proposed scheme will convert the existing redundant internal car parking area, located to the north of the existing building facing the canal into residential apartments, and construct a two-
storey rooftop extension to provide duplex apartments on top of the existing building.

The proposed car park conversion will create four duplex apartments located to the rear of the existing building facing the canal. It is considered that this is an ideal location, away from traffic
noise and pollution to create residential accommodation. A recessed second internal window is proposed to minimise the possible disturbance caused by people walking along the canal. Larger
sized windows for the interior are proposed to maximise the flow of natural light into the dwellings without allowing visibility from the exterior.

The proposed two-storey roof top extension will create nine duplex apartments. The proposed roof extension has been set back from the parapet of Barclay House, sloping back towards the
centre of the building to minimise the impact on the street scene, and to preserve the definition of the virtual boundary that characterises the existing building. The proposed new floors will fill the
gap existing in this circumstance, making the entire building a compact and completed block.

It is considered that the proposed design will enhance the existing building and the heritage assets within the vicinity of Barclay House, however as previously noted in this document there are
no designated heritage assets within the site boundaries of Barclay House, and Barclay House is not included on the local list or identified as a non-designated heritage asset. Due to the
scheme’s thoughtful design and the location of the proposed conversion and roof top extension they will not have a detrimental impact on the setting of the listed buildings close to Barclay
House.

The design of the proposed roof top extension has been carefully considered, and has evolved from initial schemes through to the final design; following discussions with the council, studying
similar extensions in historic areas, and observing the design of buildings within the vicinity of Barclay House.

The proposed scheme will provide high quality, contextually led design which produces a high quality residential development in a sustainable location close to the centre of Manchester.

It is considered that the proposed development will provide high-quality residential apartments in a sustainable location, which are apposite for the sites location. The development has been
designed to respond to the scale and massing of the site context, the existing buildings architecture and the design of adjacent buildings to produce a well-designed residential development,
which compiles with both national and local planning policies.
